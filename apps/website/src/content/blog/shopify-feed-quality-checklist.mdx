Merchants searching for **shopify feed quality checklist** usually have a live store problem, not a research assignment. This guide turns that problem into a selection and QA workflow, then explains where Klyna Feed Doctor fits without pretending an app can guarantee the business outcome.

## Short answer

> A Shopify product feed quality check should compare catalog fields with the actual item and landing page: identifier, brand, title, description, image, variant, price, availability, and URL. Fix the source data when possible instead of patching the same error separately in every channel.

## Search demand and platform context

Klyna's earlier search research found recurring intent around Shopify GTIN checks, missing identifier fixes, Merchant Center feed errors, and product data quality. These searches are operational: merchants usually arrive after a disapproval or before a paid Shopping launch.

Google's product data rules require or recommend different identifiers according to the product. A GTIN should be submitted only when the manufacturer assigned one; inventing a barcode is worse than leaving an inapplicable field empty and using the correct brand or MPN rules.

## What to check before choosing an app

| Check | What to inspect | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | GTIN, brand, MPN, SKU, and item group | Identifiers must describe the real manufacturer and variant. |
| Offer | Price, currency, condition, and availability | Feed data must agree with the landing page and purchasable offer. |
| Content | Specific title, useful description, and variant attributes | Generic or stuffed text reduces matching quality and shopper clarity. |
| Media | Stable crawlable image for the correct product or variant | Image errors can block or weaken product discovery. |
| Diagnostics | Issue reason, affected items, source field, and recheck | A fix queue should lead back to Shopify data the team can own. |

## Setup and QA workflow

1. Export the affected item IDs and exact Merchant Center issue reasons.
2. Match each item to the Shopify product and variant that owns the source data.
3. Verify whether the manufacturer assigned a valid GTIN; never invent one.
4. Align title, image, price, availability, condition, and landing-page content.
5. Regenerate or resync the feed, then wait for the channel's documented processing window.
6. Track recurring errors by supplier, import source, product type, or missing workflow.

## Common mistakes

- Using the store name as the brand when the store did not manufacture the product.
- Reusing one GTIN across different variants.
- Changing the feed while leaving contradictory price or availability on the landing page.
- Stuffing feed titles with keywords instead of describing the product accurately.

## FAQ

### Should every Shopify product have a GTIN?

No. Use a GTIN when the manufacturer assigned one and the product type requires it. Do not invent or reuse identifiers for custom or genuinely identifier-free products.

### Why does Merchant Center disagree with Shopify?

The feed can be stale, mapped incorrectly, variant data may differ, or the landing page may expose a different price, availability, currency, image, or product identity.

### Can Feed Doctor replace a feed app?

Its first job is diagnosis and catalog cleanup. A merchant may still need a channel connector for submission, scheduling, country rules, or advanced feed transformations.
