## Find the source before fixing the markup

Do not delete a JSON-LD block because it looks duplicated in a crawler report. First capture the product URL, rendered page source, and each `Product` node's name, offer, review, and `@id` values. Then identify whether the theme, an app embed, a review app, or a custom snippet produced each node. The right fix is often to configure one source of truth, not to add a third markup app.

Google requires structured data to represent visible, current page content and does not guarantee a rich result even for valid markup. Its [general structured-data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) and [Product documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-snippet) are the test: a product page should have accurate product information, and ratings, price, availability, or offers must not be invented.

| Finding | Investigate | Fix only after confirming |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two Product nodes | Theme output and every app that emits product markup | Which node is complete and matches the visible product |
| Stale price or availability | Variant selection, product data, and app cache | The live page and feed have the same current facts |
| Review markup without visible reviews | Review widget placement and merchant policy | The review content is genuinely shown to shoppers |
| Product markup on a collection | Template intent and markup source | Whether the page focuses on a single product |

Validate the edited URL in the Rich Results Test, then inspect the live mobile product page before closing the ticket. Shopify's [theme app extension documentation](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/online-store/theme-app-extensions) explains why app blocks and embeds are useful control points for merchant-managed storefront changes.
