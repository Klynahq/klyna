## Short answer

> A Shopify SEO audit app is useful when every score leads back to a real page, a visible issue, and a safe next action. It should separate metadata, headings, images, links, schema, canonicals, performance signals, keywords, and AI-search readiness instead of compressing unrelated checks into one unexplained number.

## What to check before choosing an app

| Check | What to inspect | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page coverage | Products, collections, pages, articles, and representative templates | A homepage-only scan misses most store-level problems. |
| Evidence | The URL, field, markup, or response behind each finding | A score without evidence cannot be reviewed or fixed safely. |
| Schema truth | Markup matches visible products, prices, ratings, and content | Unsupported structured data can make reports look better while reducing trust. |
| Prioritization | Severity plus likely impact and effort | Merchants need the next useful fix, not hundreds of equal warnings. |
| Write safety | Preview, scope, and confirmation for edits | Bulk metadata or theme changes need a clear rollback path. |

## Setup and QA workflow

1. Run a store-wide audit and save the starting score as a baseline, not a verdict.
2. Open the highest-severity finding and confirm it on the live URL.
3. Group repeated findings by template before editing individual products one by one.
4. Choose a reversible fix and preview any write action.
5. Recheck the URL, structured data, mobile rendering, internal links, and search appearance.
6. Record the change date, then monitor Search Console and store outcomes instead of chasing a perfect score.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a score increase as proof of ranking or revenue growth.
- Adding duplicate Product or AggregateRating schema beside a theme or review app.
- Bulk-rewriting titles without preserving product intent and brand clarity.
- Installing a speed or SEO app before identifying old scripts and app residue.

## FAQ

### Does Shopify already handle technical SEO?

Shopify handles important foundations such as sitemaps, robots files, SSL, and canonical tags. Stores can still have weak metadata, content, headings, links, schema conflicts, redirects, image issues, and app-related performance problems.

### Should I fix every audit warning?

No. Confirm the issue on a real URL, group template-level problems, and prioritize changes that improve a useful page or reduce a clear risk.

### Can an SEO app guarantee AI-search visibility?

No. Clear entities, useful answers, accurate schema, strong source pages, and crawlable internal links can improve readiness, but no app can guarantee citation by an answer engine.
